THE THOUGHT IS PART OF THE GIFT.

Feeling seen
beats being surprised.

A starting point for thoughtful giving, with room for the person you actually know.

Notice the everyday things.

The Gottman Institute describes responding to small bids for attention as a relationship practice. Our interpretation for gifts: notice what someone keeps mentioning, and start there.

Read the relationship guidance ↗
Give them something to look forward to.

Experiments by Cindy Chan and Cassie Mogilner found that experiential gifts could strengthen recipients’ feelings of connection more than material gifts. That is a group-level finding, not a promise for any particular couple.

Our take: consider a class or an afternoon plan they would actually enjoy. Check dates, accessibility and preferences first.

Read the gift-giving research ↗
Let the relationship set the pace.

Our editorial advice: a new relationship may call for something light and easy to accept. A longer relationship gives you more shared history to draw on. Ask if you are unsure; a gift is not a test of affection or a substitute for a conversation.

A small project,
for more thoughtful giving.

I’m building Little Gift Finder as a personal coding project: a place to explore gift ideas by interests, occasion and the relationship you share.

This first version uses a small, hand-written collection of ideas and simple matching rules. It does not use AI to assess you, diagnose personalities or predict relationship outcomes.

About the planned Etsy connection

I plan to apply for Etsy API access to display relevant public listing details, such as titles, images, prices and shop names, with links back to Etsy. This feature is not connected or approved yet. The project currently has no checkout, paid features or affiliate links.
